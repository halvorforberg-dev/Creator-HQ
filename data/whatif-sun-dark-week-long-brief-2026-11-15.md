## Core premise and safe scientific interpretation

Imagine that the Sun remains present with the same mass and gravity but stops emitting light and radiant energy for seven consecutive days. This is fictional. Earth's orbit remains essentially unchanged. Already-traveling sunlight continues for roughly eight minutes twenty seconds. Solar energy disappears, daytime gets dark, Moon reflected sunlight fades after its respective light-travel delay. Earth cools but doesn't freeze instantly or entirely in a week; local temperatures and power effects vary. Photosynthesis pauses; effects on plants, animals, grids and food systems are uneven. It would be a serious disruption, not reliably an extinction event. No fabricated precise global temperature, death or grid-collapse statistics. On restart light also takes travel time to reach Earth.

3. **Day 1: shock.** “Cities still glow, but daylight is gone. Solar panels stop producing power. Plants cannot use sunlight to make food. 4. **Days 2–3: adaptations.** “Cooling becomes a growing concern, especially where heating and electricity are vulnerable. People switch to stored power and other energy sources; communities shelter and adapt. Not every location is affected equally.” Practical sequences with geographic variation; no instant worldwide blackout.
5. **Days 4–6: longer-term pressure.** “A missing sunrise turns from a strange sky into a difficult emergency. There’s less incoming energy every day, more pressure on power and heating, and increasing disruption for farms and ecosystems. It isn't instant extinction, but the costs are very real.” Fresh visual developments; avoid repetitive collapsing-city montages.
6. **Day 7: what survives.** “Would the planet be a frozen ball by now? No. Heat stored in land and oceans doesn't disappear overnight. And the Earth hasn't flown off course: the Sun’s gravity never stopped.” Animated orbit and thermal-mass diagrams.
7. **The return.** “At the end of the week the Sun turns back on. Again, light needs about eight minutes to get to Earth. And when it finally reaches us, daylight returns—but the disruption doesn't disappear in a single sunrise.” First light washes across buildings and Earth.
8. **Clear payoff.** “So could we survive a week without sunlight? Humanity would likely survive, though with serious hardship. The real danger isn't that Earth instantly freezes or drifts away.
