## Pre-credit story test
**Single learning objective:** A shadow changes when the direction or amount of sunlight changes, even while the object casting it stays in place. A stationary toy's shadow can be traced and compared later.

**Opening question (first 0–2s):** Why won't Milo's long shadow fit inside his chalk circle, and has it started running away?

**Final scene's visible answer:** The same unmoved wooden toy stands between two chalk shadow outlines—a long blue morning trace and a shorter yellow later-day trace. The new real shadow now matches the later trace. Milo says the sunlight changed, not the toy or his friendship with the shadow.

**Three different story approaches considered before the decision:** (A) Observe one stationary toy's changing shadow across the day, with a humorous chalk-circle problem (chosen: clearest causal comparison); (B) A cloud makes a shadow fade and return (too little story for four minutes on its own; used as a brief supporting observation); (C) A flashlight shadow puppet party indoors (visual cause is simpler but changes the agreed outdoor sunlight-learning premise; keep for another episode).

**Five truthful, child-friendly first-frame hooks:** (1) Milo hops into a chalk circle and his extremely long morning shadow stretches out (selected, immediate physical conflict); (2) Miko's chalk outline no longer matches a stationary toy; (3) Pip's blue-wing shadow suddenly elongates; (4) a cloud briefly makes the friends' shadows fade; (5) two different chalk lines surround the same toy. Option 1 gives a visible curiosity gap that the final frame actually resolves.

**Preflight guardrails:** All three cast consistent shadows **away from the Sun**, not in arbitrary directions; sunrise/morning versus later is shown with a meaningful time transition and one fixed toy. Shadows should be long with low-angle light and shorter with higher-angle light; exact day/latitude geometries must be checked visually. Do **not** show children looking directly at the Sun. Avoid claiming clouds or changing daylight are magic. Source: https://www.schoolsobservatory.org/things-to-do/why-do-shadows-change-over-time and https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/sundial-lands-on-mars/ .

### Scene 4 — 0:58–1:21 (flexible editorial guide)

**Audio and pacing:** Music softens under simple observation.
